# Introduction {#sec:introduction}

Vision-language models (VLMs) sometimes describe objects that are not in the image. This object hallucination has been measured in image captions [@rohrbach2018object] and, more often, with yes/no probes such as "Is there a {object} in the image?", which several early models answered "yes" almost every time [@li2023evaluating]. On chest radiographs, medical devices are a natural test object. They are common ("support devices" is positive in 56.4% of CheXpert training studies [@irvin2019chexpert]), they are discrete, so presence has a definite answer, and endotracheal-tube measurements in generated reports are already corrected by a dedicated tool [@heiman2025factchexcker]. Devices also occur together, so a model may call an endotracheal tube (ETT) present because the film looks like an intensive-care study, not because it shows a tube. Stage 1 asks which of the two drives the answer of a small open VLM to "Is an endotracheal tube present in this chest X-ray?": the pixels or the prior.

We study open models with 2-4 billion parameters, whose weights are public and fit on a single GPU; a 4B medical model, MedGemma-1.5-4B, already appears in several chest X-ray benchmarks [@lee2026mccxr; @choi2026chexpercept; @lotfinia2026vision]. Their device answers are poorly mapped: we found no published device-existence results for 2B generalist models, and on the four-option tubes-and-lines questions of ReXVQA, MedGemma-4B scored 83.86% while Phi-3.5-vision (4.2B) scored 32.10%, close to the 25% chance level [@pal2025rexvqa].

Scoring matters too. Kalai et al. [@kalai2026evaluating] (Nature 2026; preprint arXiv 2509.04664) argue that accuracy-only grading rewards guessing, because a wrong answer and "I don't know" both score zero, and propose stating a confidence target t in the instructions, with a penalty of t/(1-t) points per wrong answer. For device existence, three pieces are missing. Comparable device-absent images are scarce: the closest device study had only three device-free radiographs among 4,813 [@guzel2026performance]. We found no device-existence evaluation that states a penalty for wrong answers. Finally, expert-logit fusion has been reported without controls that separate visual evidence from a plain shift in the yes-rate [@zhang2026ccd], although such shifts can explain apparent gains [@yin2025mirage].

Stage 1 instantiates, for one device, the framework of Alshahrani and Behzad [@alshahrani2026hallucination], which organises hallucination in medical imaging AI into taxonomy, detection and mitigation. Public NIH ChestX-ray14 images [@wang2017chestxray8] are matched to RANZCR CLiP labels (43 trained labellers: radiologists, radiology trainees, hospital medical officers) [@tang2021clip]. For each selected patient, an ETT-present image (A) is paired with the same patient's nearest frontal study without an ETT (B), a within-patient contrast that needs no image editing; ETT-absent images of other patients (U) and text-only and grey-image rows measure the prior. Qwen3-VL-2B [@bai2025qwen3vl] and MedGemma-1.5-4B [@sellergren2026medgemma] answer a binary prompt (P0), the same question with an "I don't know" option (P1), and P1 preceded by the confidence-target sentence of Kalai et al. at t = 0.9, with t substituted as a percentage, so that a mistake costs 9 points (P2-hi), scored as Effective Reliability [@whitehead2022reliable]. Detector-gated answers add a MedSigLIP zero-shot ETT score [@sellergren2025medgemma] to each model's yes/no log-odds and are compared with detector-alone, shuffled-expert and yes-rate-matched constant-bias controls.

Our contributions are three, each stated against its nearest precedent:

1. To our knowledge, the first credential-free, edit-free same-patient device-present/absent benchmark for a hallucination test on public pixels. Chest ImaGenome [@wu2021chest] and Medical-Diff-VQA [@hu2023expert] hold same-patient MIMIC-CXR pairs with NLP-derived labels under a credentialed licence; MC-CXR [@lee2026mccxr] and MI-CXR [@cho2026micxr] test prior-image context, not device existence.
2. To our knowledge, the first device-existence evaluation scored with the Kalai confidence-target penalty, on same-patient negatives. Blind-image rows follow Lotfinia et al. [@lotfinia2026vision]; the ETT yes/no question follows TAIX-VQA [@moll2026evaluating], which already asked it of MedGemma-4B.
3. To our knowledge, the first expert-logit fusion applied to device-existence questions on chest X-rays, reported with detector-alone, shuffled-expert and yes-rate-matched constant-bias controls. CCD [@zhang2026ccd] fuses pathology-classifier or MedSigLIP logits for report generation and VQA and reports a random-expert test, but no detector-alone or matched-bias control.
