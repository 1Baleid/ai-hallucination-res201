# 3. Benchmark construction

## 3.1 Data sources and terms

Pixels came from NIH ChestX-ray14, the NIH Clinical Center's public release of 112,120 frontal chest radiographs [@wang2017chestxray8]. We used its 1024-pixel PNG files under the release's attribution requirements: the NIH Clinical Center is acknowledged as the data provider, the CVPR 2017 paper is cited and the NIH download site is linked [@nihcc2017chestxray]. Models saw only these images.

Device labels came from the RANZCR CLiP competition [@ranzcr2021ranzcr; @tang2021clip]: [[NUM:yield.n_images|int]] radiographs of [[NUM:yield.n_patients|int]] patients in the competition's `train.csv`, all ChestX-ray14 images upscaled to their original size and saved as JPEG [@tang2021clip]. We use RANZCR CLiP labels (43 trained labellers: radiologists, radiology trainees, hospital medical officers). A random review of 5,100 consensus labels found about 15% discrepancies [@tang2021clip]. Endotracheal tube (ETT) presence was any ETT label: normal, borderline or abnormal. The labellers put every tracheostomy tube in the "ETT normal" category [@tang2021clip], so an ETT-present image may hold only a tracheostomy tube; Section 3.4 removes such images. Other labelled lines were nasogastric tubes (NGT; incompletely imaged counted as present and flagged), central venous catheters and Swan-Ganz catheters. The NIH report-mined finding labels served as covariates only, and no ground truth came from a detector. The competition rules permit use of the data for non-commercial academic research and education and forbid copying, redistribution and re-identification (Section 7.A, checked against the live Rules tab on 2 November 2026).

## 3.2 Matching the two releases

The only linkage in the study joined two public de-identified releases of the same radiographs by image content; no person was identified or sought. The competition's first-place team already used image hashing to find RANZCR images among the NIH radiographs [@alldataareext2021first], so the matching is an artefact, not a contribution. It ran in private Kaggle notebooks, and RANZCR hashes never left Kaggle.

A dimension key restricted the candidates for a JPEG to the NIH images whose recorded original width and height equal the JPEG's size. The key was pre-specified for use only if at least half of 200 sampled JPEG sizes occurred among the NIH original sizes, and otherwise every NIH image would be a candidate; [[NUM:match.dimkey_share_found|pct]] of the sample did (key used: [[NUM:match.dimkey_used]]). A JPEG whose size bucket was empty was compared with every NIH image. Each image received a 256-bit DCT perceptual hash [@zauner2010implementation]. The margin rule accepted the closest candidate when its Hamming distance was within an absolute cut and at least 16 bits below the runner-up's. The cut, calibrated at the valley of the bimodal best-distance histogram (Otsu's method [@otsu1979threshold], at most 64 bits), was [[NUM:match.cut_bits]] bits. A normalised cross-correlation (NCC) of at least 0.95 between 128-pixel thumbnails confirmed each match; when the margin failed, the best of the five closest candidates by NCC was accepted if its NCC was at least 0.95 and at least 0.02 above the next. When several JPEGs claimed one NIH file, the closest kept it and the others were excluded, and a match that disagreed with its RANZCR patient's majority NIH patient was excluded too, so each RANZCR patient maps to one NIH patient.

[[NUM:match.n_matched|int]] images were matched (rate [[NUM:match.rate|pct]]); [[NUM:match.n_ambiguous|int]] were ambiguous and excluded (within-patient share [[NUM:match.ambiguous_within_patient_share]]). A visual spot check of 60 random matched pairs, shown side by side in Kaggle, found [[NUM:match.spotcheck_correct]] of 60 correct.

## 3.3 Items

*Pairs.* A patient was eligible with at least one matched ETT-present and one ETT-absent radiograph ([[NUM:pairs.eligible_patients|int]] patients). For each of 120 randomly selected patients, A was a random ETT-present image and B the ETT-absent image nearest to A in NIH follow-up number (ties at random). Each pair is a within-patient contrast: the same patient with and without the tube. B followed A in [[NUM:pairs.n_b_after_a]] of [[NUM:pairs.n]] pairs, and the median gap was [[NUM:pairs.gap_median]] follow-up steps (interquartile range [[NUM:pairs.gap_q1]] to [[NUM:pairs.gap_q3]]). Two rungs were pre-specified for a short yield, a second disjoint pair per patient (with patient clustering) and then 80 pairs; the design ran on the [[NUM:pairs.rung]] rung.

*U items.* Other patients gave one ETT-absent image each, 120 in all under every rung. The protocol split them by the number of other labelled lines (NGT, CVC or Swan-Ganz), with the same numbers of AP and of PA images in each stratum and the AP share set to that of the B arm where availability allowed. RANZCR images were selected for catheters, so the protocol fixed a substitute in advance: with fewer than 30 patients whose ETT-absent image carries no other labelled line, the H1 contrast becomes two or more other lines versus exactly one. The yield table showed only [[NUM:yield.ett_absent_noline_patients|int]] such patients before the protocol was frozen, so the substitute applied: U held [[NUM:u.ge2.n|int]] images with two or more other labelled lines and [[NUM:u.eq1.n|int]] with exactly one, and [[NUM:u.noline.n|int]] no-line images are reported descriptively.

*Development pool.* 300 further images (150 ETT present, 150 absent), one per patient, set the detector threshold and the fusion-weight sensitivity row and supplied the 20 smoke-test images (10 per class); they entered no test statistic.

Patient sets were disjoint by NIH patient ID, every draw used a fixed seed, and the design needed 660 PNGs (360 test images). The model side saw random item IDs and NIH filenames but no arm, label or patient field.

## 3.4 Verification

Before any test-item output was opened, the student read every test image: all A, all B and all U (360 images in the standard design; [[NUM:verify.n_read|int]] read). Every A image was read because RANZCR CLiP labels tracheostomy tubes as ETTs [@tang2021clip], and the question asks about an endotracheal tube. A reading tool showed the images in one random order under read IDs unrelated to item IDs, with the arm hidden. With a one-page ETT reference sheet, the student marked each image present, absent or skip and could flag it as ambiguous, tracheostomy (the only airway tube is a tracheostomy tube) or out-of-field. The file was saved with its SHA-256 and a UTC timestamp before the join to arms; only then was model output opened. Smoke tests used development images only, so no test-item output existed before the read; the reader was blind to arm and to model output.

Exclusions followed a fixed order. A skipped or flagged image was excluded first, before any comparison with its label. Only then could a reading disagree with the RANZCR label; a disagreement was treated first as a matching error (the patient was re-matched) and, if it persisted, as label noise (the item was excluded and counted). In all, [[NUM:excl.n_items|int]] items were excluded, [[NUM:excl.flag_tracheostomy.n]] of them for a tracheostomy tube and [[NUM:excl.label_disagreement.n]] for a persistent label disagreement; Table 1 gives every reason by arm. We report the reading as agreement with the published labels: [[NUM:verify.agreement|ci]] of the non-skipped images. At least 48 h after the read ended, the student re-read 100 of the images blind in a new order (Cohen's kappa [@cohen1960coefficient] [[NUM:verify.kappa]]). No second reader was available in Stage 1, so the check measures one reader's agreement and consistency only, a stated limitation.

MedSigLIP's zero-shot ETT probability [@sellergren2025medgemma] was an automatic verifier (development-pool AUROC [[NUM:detector.dev_auroc|ci]]); its agreement with the RANZCR labels is reported per arm only, and it never excluded, flagged or selected an item.
