# Related work {#sec:related-work}

**Hallucination and presence benchmarks on chest radiographs.** MedVH tests wrongful-image, none-of-the-above and false-premise questions [@gu2026medvh]; CARES scores trustworthiness, including a post-hoc "are you sure?" question and an abstention rate [@xia2024cares]; MedHEval sorts errors by cause, has a chest X-ray subset, and finds that seven training-free mitigations help little [@chang2025medheval]. HalluCXR asks targeted yes/no finding questions, among other query types, of six systems on 856 MIMIC-CXR images, with no abstention option [@wang2026hallucxr]; ProbMed pairs each question with a negated counterpart, under which GPT-4o fell below a random baseline on finding identification [@yan2025worse]. ReXVQA scores a tubes-and-lines category in four-option form [@pal2025rexvqa]; CheXpercept finds binary lesion presence near saturation for the strongest models but reports strong response bias and no perceptual advantage for medical models [@choi2026chexpercept]; PSF-Med measures answer flips under paraphrase, including for MedGemma-1.5-4B and CheXOne [@sadanandan2026psfmed]. MC-CXR adds reliable or misleading reports and prior images as context [@lee2026mccxr], and MI-CXR also tests prior-image context [@cho2026micxr]. Device classes are absent from HalluCXR, MedHEval and CheXpercept, and most of these benchmarks draw on credentialed MIMIC-CXR data.

**Direct device precedents.** Güzel et al., "Performance of Multimodal Large Language Models in Detection and Position Assessment of Thoracic Devices on Chest Radiographs" (DOI 10.3390/diagnostics16111602) [@guzel2026performance], asked three closed models about the presence and position of the ETT, nasogastric tube, central venous catheter and Swan-Ganz catheter on 4,813 RANZCR CLiP radiographs, with a PadChest external set, prompt and repeat-run analyses and a reader study. Central venous catheters were present in 97.8% of their sample and only 3 radiographs (0.06%) had no device, so their hallucination readout on device-free images rests on three cases. TAIX-VQA (Moll et al., ML4H 2025, arXiv 2510.11196) [@moll2026evaluating] asks binary device questions of intensive-care radiographs under multimodal perturbations, including "Is an endotracheal tube present in this image?", and was run on MedGemma-4B; it has no blind-image condition and no abstention option. Lotfinia et al. (arXiv 2606.17710) [@lotfinia2026vision] test whether chest X-ray VLMs need the image, with image-swap, occlusion and image-removed rows, a support-devices question block and 224-px inputs; MedGemma-1.5-4B is among their models, and our P0 follows the default template of their MIT-licensed code. McLaughlin et al. (arXiv 2604.09841) [@mclaughlin2026is] compare Gemma-3-4B with MedGemma-4B on binary presence tasks that include CheXpert radiographs, score refusals, and find no consistent benefit from medical fine-tuning, with 20-point swings under minor rewording.

**Same-patient resources.** Chest ImaGenome adds scene graphs with tube, line and device attributes to sequential MIMIC-CXR images, derived by NLP with a manually validated gold subset [@wu2021chest]; Medical-Diff-VQA asks difference questions about pairs of MIMIC-CXR studies, with answers derived from the reports by rules [@hu2023expert]. Both require PhysioNet credentialing. CheXTemporal excludes support devices by design [@prakash2026chextemporal], and MC-CXR and MI-CXR test prior-image context rather than device existence.

**Abstention and penalised scoring.** Kalai et al. (Nature 2026; preprint arXiv 2509.04664) [@kalai2026evaluating] argue that accuracy-only grading rewards confident guessing and propose explicit confidence targets: answer only if more than t confident, with mistakes penalised t/(1-t) points. Effective Reliability scores VQA with a cost for wrong answers and zero for abstentions [@whitehead2022reliable]; with cost t/(1-t) it implements this grading. Medical precedents are partial: Med-HALT scores text-only medical questions with a penalised pointwise score (+1 correct, -0.25 wrong) [@pal2023medhalt]; HALT-MedVQA [@wu2024hallucination] and MedVH [@gu2026medvh] use none-of-the-above and false-premise questions; CARES asks for confidence after the answer [@xia2024cares]; Byun et al. tested a penalty-style prompt with 2B models on medical VQA, without an abstain option [@byun2026overconfidence]. We found no evaluation that states a penalty and offers "I don't know" on chest X-ray device questions.

**Grounding, retrieval and their controls.** CCD (Findings of ACL 2026, arXiv 2509.23379) [@zhang2026ccd] adds a clipped log-odds bias from an expert classifier to a radiology model's logits during decoding; its main expert is a pathology classifier, MedSigLIP is tested as a replacement, and a random-expert test is reported, but neither the expert alone nor a yes-rate-matched bias. Our detector-gated answers apply the same fusion rule to yes/no logits. ARCD re-weights tokens, attention and logits toward anatomical masks [@liang2026anatomical]; EVADE abstains unless full-image and zoomed answers agree, and its own ablation finds that random and centre crops barely change the results [@raiaan2026evade]. Retrieval can help or hurt: RULE [@xia2024rule] and MMed-RAG [@xia2025mmedrag] calibrate retrieval and fine-tune 7B models against over-reliance on retrieved reports, while When Retrieval Hurts [@idoko2026when] traces findings in generated reports to retrieved evidence, most often under mismatched retrieval. Contrastive decoding is a cautionary case: VCD [@leng2024mitigating] is null-to-negative on chest X-ray VQA in MedHEval [@chang2025medheval], Med-VCD [@mahdavi2026medvcd] and VGS-Decoding [@kolli2026vgsdecoding], and on general-domain probes its gains were traced to a one-directional shift toward "yes" [@yin2025mirage] that random perturbations of equal size also produce [@bendre2026rethinking]. These results motivate our shuffled-expert and constant-bias controls.

**Synthetic edits, deferred to later stages.** Editing can remove a device from the same scene, but the edit's validity must be shown. RadEdit edits chest radiographs inside masks and was used to stress-test classifiers and segmenters [@perezgarcia2024radedit]; RadCF specialises a foundation image model to produce device-removal edits, validated with classifiers and image metrics [@xing2026generating]; Xiong et al. remove annotated attributes with RadEdit and keep samples whose answer changes after a foreground edit but not after a background edit [@xiong2026rethinking]; Frid-Adar et al. inserted synthetic ETTs into real radiographs to train a detector [@fridadar2019endotracheal]. Stage 1 uses no edited image.

**Datasets.** RANZCR CLiP [@tang2021clip] re-annotates 30,083 radiographs drawn from NIH ChestX-ray14 with image-level labels for the ETT, nasogastric tube, central venous catheter and Swan-Ganz catheter, plus line tracings on a subset. Its 43 trained labellers were radiologists, radiology trainees and hospital medical officers; about 30% of the images were double-labelled, and a re-review of 5,100 consensus labels found about 15% discrepancies. Tracheostomy tubes were labelled in the "ETT normal" category rather than in a class of their own, so an ETT-present label can mark an image whose only airway tube is a tracheostomy [@tang2021clip]. NIH ChestX-ray14 [@wang2017chestxray8] releases 112,120 frontal radiographs of 30,805 patients as 1024 x 1024 PNGs with report-mined finding labels, free to use with attribution. The RANZCR images are NIH radiographs upscaled to their original resolution and saved as JPEG [@tang2021clip], so each has an NIH counterpart.

**Positioning.** Stage 1 claims three narrow additions and borrows the rest. Its device-absent images are real studies of the same patients, built from public pixels without credentials or editing; to our knowledge, this makes it the first credential-free, edit-free same-patient device-present/absent benchmark for a hallucination test (Güzel et al. had three device-free images, TAIX-VQA and Lotfinia et al. do not pair studies of the same patient, and Chest ImaGenome and Medical-Diff-VQA are credentialed and NLP-labelled). To our knowledge, it is also the first device-existence evaluation scored with the Kalai confidence-target penalty, on same-patient negatives; the ETT question (TAIX-VQA), the P0 template and blind-image rows (Lotfinia et al.), penalised text scores (Med-HALT) and Effective Reliability (Whitehead et al.) predate it. Finally, to our knowledge, it is the first application of expert-logit fusion to device-existence questions on chest X-rays with detector-alone, shuffled-expert and yes-rate-matched constant-bias controls; the fusion rule and the random-expert test come from CCD. Matching the RANZCR and NIH releases is an engineering step, not a contribution: the competition's first-place team already used image hashing to find RANZCR images among the NIH radiographs [@alldataareext2021first].
