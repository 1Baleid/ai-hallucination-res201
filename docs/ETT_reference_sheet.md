# ETT reference sheet for the blind read

A study aid for the verification read (PROTOCOL.md section 2) of frontal chest radiographs; not clinical guidance. You read as a non-expert: the labels of record are the RANZCR CLiP labels (43 trained labellers: radiologists, radiology trainees, hospital medical officers) [1], and your reading is reported as agreement with them. Judge each image on its own; the tool hides the arm and the order is random.

**Routine (about 25-35 s per image).** Find the trachea (the dark air column in the midline above the heart) and the carina (where it divides into the two main bronchi). Look for a tube inside the trachea and follow every tube to both ends. Check the neck for a tracheostomy. If a stripe is faint, press C (contrast) or Z (100% view).

## Endotracheal tube (ETT)

- It comes down from the top of the image (through the mouth or nose) and runs in the midline inside the tracheal air column, as a thin bright line or stripe, often between two faint walls; tubes carry a radiopaque strip so that they show on radiographs [2].
- The tip normally lies 5 ± 2 cm above the carina [2, 4], near the medial ends of the clavicles [4]. The carina usually projects over T5-T7; neck flexion moves the tip down and extension moves it up [2].
- Tip position does not matter here: high, low, at or below the carina, or in a main bronchus, the tube is present. The RANZCR CLiP categories "normal", "borderline" and "abnormal" all mean present, and "abnormal" includes a tip at or below the carina [1].

## Tracheostomy tube (flag 2)

- It enters through the front of the neck (the stoma), so it starts at the neck instead of coming down from above. It is short, usually curved, often with a flat plate at the skin; its tip often lies near the level of the clavicles, about midway between the insertion point and the carina [3].
- It is not an ETT. If it is the only airway tube, answer **absent** and set flag 2; if you cannot tell which tube it is, give your best answer and set flags 2 and 1. The RANZCR CLiP labellers labelled every tracheostomy tube "ETT normal" [1], so flag 2 is what keeps these images out of the ETT analysis.

## Not an ETT

- **Nasogastric or orogastric tube**: follows the oesophagus. It descends in the midline, passes the carina, crosses the diaphragm and ends below the left hemidiaphragm [5]; an ETT ends in the chest. Many ventilated patients have both.
- **ECG leads and external wires**: thin wires and round electrode buttons that cross the chest and the body outline without following the airway. Like tubing and clamps on or under the patient, they are usually recognisable for what they are [6].
- **Central lines (CVC, PICC), Swan-Ganz catheters, chest drains**: lines from the neck, shoulder or arm to the superior vena cava, with the tip near the cavoatrial junction [4], or on through the heart; drains at the side of the chest. A jugular line may run beside the trachea, never inside its air column.

## Decision rules

| Key | Answer when |
|---|---|
| **P present** | A tube runs down the tracheal air column from the top of the image and ends in the chest, at any tip position. |
| **A absent** | You can follow the trachea from the top of the image to the carina and no tube lies in it (lines beside it do not count). |
| **S skip** | You cannot judge at all: the image is missing or unreadable, is not a frontal chest radiograph, or shows no trachea. |

Flags go with an answer, never instead of one, and only when they apply: **1 ambiguous** (you answered but are not sure: faint stripe, rotation, overlapping lines, poor exposure; try C and Z first); **2 tracheostomy** (present or suspected); **3 out of field** (the top of the trachea is cut off, or a tube runs off the edge so you cannot follow it; answer from what you can see). Flagged and skipped images are excluded by the pre-registered rule, so a flag is not a way around a hard decision.

Keys: P/A/S answer, 1/2/3 flags, N note, Enter confirm, Backspace back one image (one step), Z 100% view, C contrast. The 100-image retest starts no earlier than 48 h after the read is saved.
